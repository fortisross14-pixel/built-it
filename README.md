# Built it! An architect's career

A roguelite architecture game. Start as a junior architect with a corner shop. Grades decide where your career goes next, and what every future architect can build with.

## Loop
1. **Commission board.** The first job is one of three basics: Neighbourhood Boutique, Fast-food Counter or Corner Grocery. After that the board offers jobs at your level on a 1–10 ladder, a safer job one level down or a stretch job after an A or S, plus a locked teaser two levels up. The ladder covers 28 briefs in 10 industries: boutique → fast fashion → chain flagship → luxury maison, café → bistro → fine dining, corner grocery → supermarket → food hall, gym → sports hall → arena, library → school → museum → national museum, apartments, office tower, shopping centre, airport terminal.
   - **Every offer is different.** After the first job, offers often come with:
     - **A site twist:**
       - a **narrow lot**, long and thin with shared walls on both sides, so windows only go front and back and skylights help;
       - an **infill gap** between two buildings, tiny, so you build at least 2 floors;
       - an **L-shaped lot**, where a neighbour's building takes a corner.

       Shared walls refuse windows and doors.
     - **Client wishes** (1 to 3, more at higher levels), for example: a terrace with outdoor tables, women's and men's restrooms or fitting rooms, women's and men's lines in separate rooms, a bigger kitchen, trolley-wide aisles, greenery, daylight everywhere, or spending under 90%. Wishes count toward Requirements.
     - **Tighter budgets** as the level climbs.
   - **28 briefs** including a pharmacy, a hair salon, a kindergarten, a bank branch, a neighbourhood cinema and a boutique hotel.
2. **Design 2D.**
   - The Room and Furnish tools open a catalog. Pick one thing, place as many as you like, then press the red ✕ to stop. The catalog has a **This project / All** filter.
   - Select a room to get a bubble with **Move** (the room and everything in it), **Merge** (with a same-type neighbour) and **Erase** (asks for confirmation).
   - **Move anything, any time.** Select a room or a piece of furniture and drag it.
     - Rooms keep their furniture, doors and windows when they move.
     - A room that doesn't fit yet, because it overlaps another room, leaves the lot or has nothing below it, stays red and lands by itself once its spot is free. To swap two rooms, drag one onto the other, then drag the second into the gap.
     - Red furniture works the same way.
     - You can't submit while anything is red.
   - **Furniture has a bubble** too: Rotate, Duplicate, Delete, quality and product.
   - **Floors:** the **+** button under the floor list adds a floor up to the brief's limit. The layers button duplicates or removes the top floor. Stairs and lifts show where they land on the floor above: green once linked, amber where you still need to place stairs.
   - The Erase tool only removes objects.
   - **Fixtures plus products.** Place a Clothes rack, Chiller, Aisle or Display, then assign its product (Women / Men / Kids, Produce / Meat / Dairy / Frozen / Drinks, Snacks / Condiments / Pasta & tins / Bakery...).
   - **Merchandising** counts toward Functionality: keep each department together, put displays and mannequins next to their racks and produce crates by the produce chiller, and keep tills near the door. The plan draws link lines between fixtures that work together.
3. **Decorate 3D.** Floors, wall paint, ceilings, facade, entrance doors, window joinery and roof finishes, decor and landscaping, and ceiling lights, with a day/night toggle. By day, sunlight only enters through windows, skylights and doors, so windowless rooms look dim unless their lamps are on. At night, only your lamps light the rooms. Quality tiers: Basic, Standard, Premium.
   **Requirements and nice-to-haves.**
   - While you build, the brief lists only the **requirements**: rooms and their sizes, furniture and equipment, lighting, floors, budget and the client's wishes. **Requirements** scores exactly that list, so it reaches 100 once everything is ticked.
   - Everything else is a **nice-to-have** you learn from the review: tables not too close, the counter by the kitchen, short key routes, booths, women's/men's restrooms, premium seating and finishes, curated materials. These count toward Functionality and Aesthetics, and the review lists what was good and what was missing.
4. **Review.** Functionality, Requirements, Aesthetics and Budget, averaged into a grade:
   | Grade | Score | Effect |
   |---|---|---|
   | S | ≥ 90 | +2 levels |
   | A | ≥ 80 | +1 level |
   | B | ≥ 68 | +1 level |
   | C | ≥ 55 | −1 level |
   | F | < 55 | fails: retry for a strike, or abandon the career |
   **How hard is S?** A correct, tasteful design earns about 80 in each rating. The rest is expert work:
   - **Functionality:** expert touches for the building type, for example tables spaced 1 m apart, the counter by the kitchen door, booths or sofas, premium seating, outdoor tables, unlocked specialist pieces, and every key route Close.
   - **Aesthetics:** curation, meaning premium-grade finishes, pack and signature materials, statement pieces, fine windows and doors, and facade and roof upgrades.
   - **Budget:** 70% of budget scores 100, 95% about 80, exactly on budget about 68, 10% over about 55, and 25% over under 40.
   - **S needs 90+ overall and every rating at 85+.** In practice that means a specialist profile, unlocked pieces and a lean build.
5. **Rewards.**
   - **Permanent, for all architects:** B, A and S unlock that industry's basic, advanced and special items and product lines. Examples: boutique displays and mannequins (B), shoe walls and jewellery cases (A), a VIP salon and runway (S), or drinks chillers (B), a bakery aisle, in-store bakery and deli (A) and a juice bar (S) for grocery.
   - **This architect only:** fees earn cash for supplier packs: paints, floors, facades, lighting, ceilings, entrance doors, decor and studio services (intern, contractor, mentor, renders).

Ten commissions per career, and three strikes closes the studio. Finished careers go to the legacy hall.

## Architects
Ten playable profiles. Name, age, country and gender stay editable.
- **Two studio founders** (open from the start): no rating bonus, but a founders kit with paper wallpaper and paper lanterns.
- **Eight specialists**, unlocked by completing a career (5+ approved commissions) with a founder. Each founder trains four:
  - **Background bonus:**
    - Pure: a little of everything.
    - Art: +6 Aesthetics.
    - Engineering: missed requirements cost 30% less.
    - Business: −10% construction cost.
  - **Expertise** in one or two industries (civic, housing, fashion, food, sport, health, grocery…): +5 Functionality and +10% fees on those projects.
  - **Signature finishes** that match the expertise: a wall, a floor, a ceiling and a roof only that architect can use, such as Venetian plaster, Nero marble, a barrel vault and a patinated copper roof.

## Placement and layout checks
- Any item can go in any room. A drinks station in the WC still works, but **Item placement** lowers Functionality. Items marked for outdoor use (tables, grill, displays, plants…) can also go outside on the lot. Toilets, ranges and similar can't.
- Rotation is always allowed. If a rotated item overlaps something, it turns red and doesn't count until you move it.
- **Key checks** for each building type measure walking distance through doors between what matters and rate it Close, Mid or Far. Examples: tables to the kitchen, counter next to the kitchen, fitting rooms to the shop floor, tills by the door, chillers near stock.
- **Signature features** for each type: booths, women's/men's restrooms or fitting rooms (set a room's gender in its bubble), premium seating, and premium finishes (worth less in fast food). Bonus features such as booths or prepared food in grocery add extra points.
- On the review screen, tap Functionality, Requirements, Aesthetics or Budget to see a breakdown of what earned or cost points. Examples: walls that miss the client's taste, rooms without windows, windows blocked by tall shelving, cheap furniture, missing features.

## Design
The menus use **Monograph**, a design language based on architects' presentation decks: off-white paper, floating white cards, hairlines, light/bold title pairs, large condensed numerals (Antonio), self-drawing linework, a white massing-model 3D hero, and diamond-cropped project images with numbered markers. Motion is calm: pages wipe in like deck slides, windows unfold and fold away, and lists stagger in. The plan drawing keeps its CAD mono type.

Project icons load from `public/art/projects/<id>.jpg`. See `ART_PROMPTS.md` for the image prompts and `scripts/slice-art.cjs` to cut a 6×4 grid into tiles.

## Saving
- **Every change is saved straight away.** The save is written within a fraction of a second, and immediately when you switch away from the app or close it.
- **Two copies are kept in sync.** The game may be open in two places, for example the installed app and a browser tab on Android, or two tabs. Each save carries a revision stamp, and a copy never overwrites newer progress. When a copy comes back to the front, or another copy saves, it loads the newest save and says so.
- **Two stores.** Every save goes to browser storage and to a second copy in the browser's database (IndexedDB). On start-up the newest of the two wins, so a tab that crashes before the browser finished writing doesn't lose your last moves. Nothing is written until that check is done.
- **A save that can't be read is never overwritten.** It's set aside first.
- **Earlier versions.** Settings keeps the last 20 versions, at most one a minute, and can restore any of them.
- **The editor shows when it last saved,** for example "SAVED 14:03:22".
- **The design in progress has its own copy,** so a full storage can't cost you the level you're working on. If storage fills up, older thumbnails and plans are dropped first.
- **Failures are never silent.** A **Saving failed** banner shows with **Retry** and **Download backup**.
- **Settings → Backup** downloads your save as a file and restores it. On iPhone, the installed app and Safari keep separate saves, so use a backup to move between them.
- `npm run test:e2e` runs the browser save tests: two copies, reload and background right after an edit, a long session, full storage, backup and restore, career steps, and old saves.

## Install as an app
Built it! is a PWA. Open the site on a phone and use **Settings → Install Built it!**. If the browser has no install prompt, the button shows the manual steps for your browser. On iPhone, use Safari's **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It runs fullscreen and works offline after the first visit, because the service worker caches the game and all its art. Icons are rendered by `scripts/make-icons.cjs`.

## Controls
- Plan: one finger uses the tool, two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. Mouse: wheel zooms, right-drag pans.
- 3D: drag to orbit, pinch to zoom.

## Code
